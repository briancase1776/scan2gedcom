DATA FORMAT
Structured data for the family group sheets, one JSON file per sheet:
data/<sheet-id>.json, from families/<sheet-id>.txt. The text sheets
stay the readable version; these files feed tools/build_gedcom.py,
which writes one GEDCOM 5.5.1 file for import into a family-tree
program.
Check with:  python3 -I tools/check_data.py

FILE
  {
    "sheet":    "<sheet-id>",
    "people":   [ PERSON, ... ],
    "families": [ FAMILY, ... ]
  }

  A sheet's file holds its principal, the principal's spouses, the
  principal's children (birth, death and burial only; their own
  marriages belong to their own sheets or to a lineage file) and the
  families in which the principal is husband or wife. The same person
  may appear in several files; the builder merges them by id.

  A lineage file, data/line-<sheet-id>.json from families/line-<sheet-
  id>.txt, takes a sheet's branch the rest of the way down: the
  spouses and marriages of the sheet's children, and every person
  below them in the author's numbered descendant entries, to the last
  entry. It refers to the sheet's own people by id and does not repeat
  them. Its "sheet" is "line-<sheet-id>".

  Everyone the book names as family goes in as a person, living or
  not, with the family that links them: a spouse's parents (a family
  whose child is the spouse), a step-child (in their own parents'
  family; see FAMILY), a child of an earlier marriage. Nobody is kept
  in notes only. A parent the book only guesses at ("family tradition
  says") is not linked as a parent: put the guess in the child's
  "note". One output: the living are in the same data and the same
  GEDCOM, flagged by their Living value (see LIVING).

  Layout: one fact per line, as in examples/demo/data/. It keeps diffs
  small and files easy to compare with the sheet. To change a file,
  edit its lines; never load it and write it back with a JSON library,
  which re-lays out the whole file and buries the change in a diff of
  every line.

PERSON
  {
    "id":      "harlan-abel-1801",     the id used on the sheets
    "given":   "Abel",
    "surname": "Harlan",               maiden name for women, "" if
                                       unknown
    "aka":     ["Abe Harlan"],         optional: other names and
                                       spellings
    "sex":     "M" | "F" | "U",        from the book (son, daughter, he,
                                       she, Mrs.); if it doesn't say,
                                       from a clearly male or female
                                       given name; U only if neither
    "living":  "no" | "yes" | "check", as on the sheet's Living line
                                       (see LIVING)
    "note":    "...",                  optional: about the person, not
                                       one event, with its pages
                                       (written to the GEDCOM as a NOTE)
    "facts":   [ FACT, ... ]
  }

  Use "note" for what is about the person rather than an event (a
  traditional parent, a name left blank in the book), not an EVEN
  fact such as "Son of ...".

  Names as typed. "given" and "surname" are spelled as the book types
  them for that person. When one family's name is typed two ways (one
  child "Harlan", another "Harland"), keep each as typed and list the
  difference under WHERE THE BOOK DISAGREES WITH ITSELF on the sheet;
  don't normalize surnames. The same person's other spellings go in
  "aka".

FAMILY
  {
    "id":       "fam-<husband-id>-<wife-id>",   unique
    "husband":  "<person id>" or null,
    "wife":     "<person id>" or null,
    "children": ["<person id>", ...],   in the sheet's order
    "adopted":  ["<person id>", ...],   optional: children adopted into
                                        this family by people neither
                                        of whom is their own parent,
                                        from the book's words
                                        ("adopted"); GEDCOM PEDI
                                        adopted
    "inferred": ["<person id>", ...],   optional: children placed in
                                        this family partly or wholly by
                                        working out [I], e.g. which
                                        marriage a child came from; the
                                        GEDCOM link carries a note
                                        saying so
    "step":     {"<person id>": "husband" | "wife"},
                                        optional: step-children in this
                                        family, each naming which
                                        parent is the step-parent (see
                                        below)
    "facts":    [ FACT, ... ]           MARR, DIV
  }

  Every id in "adopted", "inferred" and "step" is also in "children".
  An inferred placement can be one parent named by the book and the
  other worked out, or both worked out; the sheet or lineage file says
  which parent, and how.

  A step-child is a child of their own parents' family (a parent may
  be null if not named). They may also go in the step-family, listed
  in its "children" and in its "step", which names the step-parent:
  "step": {"pike-silas-1855": "husband"}. They are never a plain child
  of a step-parent. A step-parent's adoption is still "step": the
  adoption is an EVEN fact on the child ("Adopted by his stepfather")
  and any new name an "aka". "adopted" is only for a child adopted by
  people neither of whom is their own parent; a child is never both
  "step" and "adopted". In the GEDCOM, the step-family link carries a
  note naming the step-parent, and under the family's CHIL line the
  per-parent tags Ancestry reads: _FREL / _MREL "Step" on the
  step-parent's side, "Natural" on the other ("Adopted" on both for
  an adopted child). This keeps the line of descent clear for DNA
  matching.

FACT
  {
    "type":   "BIRT" | "DEAT" | "BURI" | "MARR" | "DIV" | "RESI" |
              "OCCU" | "RELI" | "EVEN",
    "date":   "4 MAR 1801",    optional, GEDCOM form (see DATES)
    "place":  "Linden Co.",    optional, as the sheet gives it
    "value":  "...",           OCCU / RELI / EVEN: what it was
    "kind":   "F" | "R" | "Q" | "I",   the sheet's tag
    "cite":   ["PDF 2", "PDF 14"],     the book pages ("PDF 2-3" for a
                                       range); may be empty only for I
    "record": "rec-...",       optional: the record file it rests on
    "note":   "..."            optional: the book's own words, a
                               qualifier, or the reasoning for an [I]
  }

  Where the book disagrees with itself, write one fact per reading,
  each with its own pages. Don't pick one.

  A guess is not a vital event. The author's "possible", "perhaps" or
  "rumored", or a place or date worked out on the sheet, goes in as an
  EVEN fact whose value says so ("Possible death", "Possible burial",
  "Possible birthplace", "Rumored residence"), never as BIRT, DEAT,
  BURI or RESI, so a tree program doesn't show it as fact.

MERGING
  The builder merges a person's facts from all files, dropping exact
  duplicates only. When the same event is in two files (a child in the
  parents' file and in their own), the two fact objects must be
  identical, or the event appears twice in the tree. If the child's
  sheet has the better version (a record link, a fuller note), change
  it in both files. check_data.py warns about near-duplicates.

DATES  (GEDCOM 5.5.1)
  4 MAR 1801      MAR 1801     1801
  ABT 1774        BEF 1 MAR 1869     AFT 1820     EST 1800    CAL 1801
  BET 1833 AND 1834     FROM 1819 TO 1851     FROM 1849     TO APR 1846
  (17 Sep)        a date phrase: the book's words, when there is no
                  year
  INT 1950 (clipping dated Mar. 1950)
                  a date worked out, with the reason in brackets
  Months: JAN FEB MAR APR MAY JUN JUL AUG SEP OCT NOV DEC.
  A date that won't fit (a typing slip, a "[?]" reading) goes in as
  the best GEDCOM date with the book's text in "note"; if there is no
  sensible date, leave "date" out and put the text in "note". The
  words in brackets may not themselves contain brackets.

LIVING
  The sheet's Living line, and "living" here, follow one rule:
    "no"     if the person is dead (a death or burial is given), or
             was born 1915 or earlier, or, with no birth year, married
             before 1940, had a child born before 1940, or had a
             spouse born before 1900 [I];
    "check"  otherwise;
    "yes"    only where the book says the person is living.
  So nobody born in the 1800s is marked living. Give the reason on the
  Living line: "no [I]: married 1874", "check: born 1941".
  The years are a choice, not a fact: agree them with the user. They
  are set in scan2gedcom.json, and these are the defaults if it is
  left out:
    "living_rule": {"born_by": 1915, "married_before": 1940,
                    "child_born_before": 1940, "spouse_born_before": 1900}
  check_data.py warns about anyone whose "living" doesn't follow the
  rule. "check" and "yes" only add a privacy flag (RESN privacy);
  nobody is left out for being living. The project and the tree stay
  private.

RULES
  Every fact comes from the sheet and carries its pages. Nothing is
  added that the sheet doesn't say, and nothing the book gives is left
  out to save time: every record the book copies or abstracts (an
  obituary, a notice, a deed) gets its record file, on a lineage file
  as on a sheet.
